'It helps a lot': Charlotte program trains aspiring truck drivers in face of national shortage, Trump restrictions

5h ago · Published Nov 23, 2026 · United States
Reported by WFAE Read the original article at WFAE ›
Share

A training program run by NCIA in Charlotte currently enrolls about a dozen people in a commercial driver's license course. The Baltimore-based group serves people who face employment barriers, and through a partnership with Transtech CDL Training, enrollees get about four weeks of instruction. People in hardship, such as food stamp recipients and survivors of domestic violence, have their license costs covered, which can otherwise run five to six thousand dollars.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects about 214,500 openings a year over the next decade as drivers retire or leave the field. In February, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy issued a rule that makes immigrants ineligible for CDLs when states cannot check their driving records. Duffy said the rule closes a loophole that lets non-citizens obtain CDLs with federal Employment Authorization Documents.
