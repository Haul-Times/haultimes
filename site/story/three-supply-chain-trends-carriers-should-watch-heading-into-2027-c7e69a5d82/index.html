Three supply chain trends carriers should watch heading into 2027

2 days ago · Published Nov 19, 2026 · United States
Reported by FleetOwner Read the original article at FleetOwner ›
Share

In a FleetOwner column, the chief executive of a Lincoln, Nebraska freight brokerage outlines three supply chain trends he expects fleet owners to watch in 2027. He cites Bureau of Labor Statistics data showing that trucking has lost more than 100,000 jobs since 2023, although the decline slowed in 2026. Using mileage and fuel-economy figures from the American Transportation Research Institute and Department of Energy diesel prices from early 2026 onward, he estimates that higher diesel costs add roughly $40,000 a year per truck, or $400,000 for a 10-truck fleet. He warns that if a recession pushed rates below operating costs, some carriers would be forced to haul freight at a loss. He also argues that driver shortages will keep pressure on pay, which fuel costs make harder to fund.
